# Task: Write the chapter summary

Chapter title: {title}

Below are notes on this chapter, one set per chunk, in chapter order. Turn them into **one** integrated summary, not a summary per chunk.

## Rules
- Use only the notes. If two notes conflict, keep both versions and say so briefly.
- Merge events and character notes that repeat across chunks.
- A later chunk may finish a scene or answer a question that an earlier one left open; report as an open thread only what is still open at the end of the chapter.
- Keep events in the order the chapter presents them, and mention flashbacks and viewpoint shifts.
- Use only quotes that appear in the notes, copied exactly.
- Aim for 350–600 words, fewer for a short chapter; **never exceed 900 words**. To save space, drop minor events before the twists, reveals, and causal links that hold the plot together.
- Use exactly the seven sections below, with these headings and numbers. If the notes have nothing for a section, write "Not specified." under it rather than padding it.
- Output only the summary, as Markdown without a code fence.

## Output structure

# {title}

## 1. Synopsis
One short paragraph: what happens in this chapter and how the situation at the end differs from the start. Do not presume knowledge of the rest of the book.

## 2. Plot Events
A numbered list of the key events in chapter order. Each item is one or two sentences saying who does what and what follows from it.

## 3. Characters
For each significant character, a `### Name` heading, then their actions, their motives as shown in the text, and how they or their relationships change in this chapter.

## 4. Setting and World
Where and when the chapter takes place, the atmosphere, and any world-building details introduced.

## 5. Themes and Motifs
Themes, symbols, and recurring images, each tied to a specific moment in the chapter. Include the narrative voice (point of view, tone) when it shapes the meaning.

## 6. Notable Lines
Up to three short quotes from the notes, each with a sentence on why it matters.

## 7. Open Threads
Unanswered questions, unresolved conflicts, and possible foreshadowing that a reader should carry into the next chapter.
